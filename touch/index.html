The sleep light

There’s a third message, and it was on this page before you woke it. The sleep light wasn’t breathing. It was blinking Morse code.

−T·−A·−−·P

It was telling you how to wake it.

What’s real

The sleep work in the email is real. My M2 Pro MacBook Pro runs Aurora Silicon Linux, and it couldn’t truly sleep: with the lid closed, Linux drew about 1.4 W, where macOS sleeps at about 0.24 W.

  • October 15, 2026First commit to Aurora-DeepIdle, research into deep idle on the M2 Pro (T6020, J414s).
  • November 18First real suspend-to-RAM sleep on the M2 Pro, woken by its alarm.
  • November 20Linux back in userspace after a real suspend-to-RAM.

That’s a little over a month from the first commit to waking up. It works on a test boot today; making lid close just work every day is the last stretch. Live status and test results.

The next machine

The touchscreen MacBook Pro runs on the M5 Pro and M5 Max. Linux already boots the non-touch 16-inch M5 Pro (J716s), with the display, keyboard, trackpad, GPU, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and suspend-to-idle working, according to the m4-m5-linux tracker as of November 8.

The tracker has no row for a touchscreen, and no M5 has real suspend-to-RAM yet. Those are the two things I’d work on: teach it to feel a touch, and teach it to sleep.
